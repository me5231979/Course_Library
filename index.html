For all Vanderbilt staff

AI Basics

A working picture of AI from the ground up: what it actually is, how machines learn, how language models predict, how to prompt well and verify the output, which Vanderbilt tools fit which data, and a personal one-week plan to put AI on real work.

  • 9 sections · 6 simulators + prompt lab
  • 60 or 90 minute paths
  • In person, virtual, or self-paced
Full course description (printable)
Learning objectives
  1. Distinguish AI, machine learning, and generative AI, and spot each in the tools you already use
  2. Explain how a language model produces an answer, and why the same question gives different answers
  3. Construct four-part prompts that get useful, reliable results on real work
  4. Evaluate AI output before trusting it, and route tasks to the right Vanderbilt tool by data sensitivity
  5. Create a one-week plan that puts AI on one of your own tasks, and keeps the time it saves

For people managers & team leads

Navigating Difficult Conversations

A shared toolkit for the conversations managers avoid: the three conversations inside every hard exchange, SBI feedback, STATE openers, trust built with BRAVING, Radical Candor, recovery moves for tears, anger, and silence, and a planned real conversation within seven days.

  • 8 sections · 7 interactive tools
  • 60 or 90 minute paths
  • In person, virtual, or self-paced
Full course description (printable)
Learning objectives
  1. Name the three conversations hiding inside any hard exchange, and spot the one that trips you up
  2. Turn a vague concern into specific, fair feedback using the SBI model
  3. Open a candid conversation in a way that keeps the other person safe, using STATE
  4. Assess your own trust-building behaviors with the BRAVING inventory and pick one to strengthen
  5. Plan one real conversation you've been avoiding, and commit to having it within seven days

For people managers & team leads

Coaching for Performance

Building managers who grow people, not just manage tasks: the research case for coaching (Google's Project Oxygen), the GROW model practiced against a virtual coachee, powerful questions, Goldsmith's Feedforward, and a real coaching conversation planned with a written opening question.

  • 7 sections · 6 interactive tools + GROW simulator
  • 60 or 90 minute paths
  • In person, virtual, or self-paced
Full course description (printable)
Learning objectives
  1. Explain why coaching, not directing, is the top predictor of great management, and spot your own telling default
  2. Structure a full coaching conversation with the GROW model, from goal to committed action
  3. Convert closed, leading, and advice-loaded lines into open, powerful questions
  4. Give and receive future-focused Feedforward suggestions without judgment or defense
  5. Choose your coaching model and commit to one real coaching conversation this week, opening question written

For AI Basics graduates · 201 level

AI 201: Beyond the Basics

Under the prompt box: why models forget (context and attention), how RAG grounds answers in real sources, choosing between prompting, RAG, and fine-tuning, four advanced prompt patterns, how AI agents fail, and the governance habits, hallucination-spotting and data tiering, that make it all safe to run.

  • 7 sections · 7 interactive tools + 2 simulators
  • 60 or 90 minute paths · requires AI Basics
  • In person, virtual, or self-paced
Full course description (printable)
Learning objectives
  1. Explain how context and attention shape an answer, and why long conversations lose the thread
  2. Describe what Retrieval-Augmented Generation fixes, and what it doesn't
  3. Choose between prompting, RAG, and fine-tuning, weighing cost, data, and maintenance
  4. Apply four advanced prompt patterns: Few-Shot, Chain-of-Thought, Template, Flipped Interaction
  5. Recognize the agent loop, name its failure modes, and place the human checkpoint
  6. Classify information into governance tiers and build one governed AI workflow

For all Vanderbilt staff & leaders

Emotional Intelligence & Interpersonal Skills

Lead yourself before you lead others: the research case for EI (Goleman, Google's Project Aristotle), the four domains, mapping your blind spots with the Johari Window, the neuroscience of naming emotions, active listening scored against a rubric, Rosenberg's NVC reframe, and saying hard things with candor and care.

  • 7 sections · 7 interactive tools + private Johari mapper
  • 60 or 90 minute paths
  • In person, virtual, or self-paced
Full course description (printable)
Learning objectives
  1. Explain Goleman's four EI domains and map your own blind spots with the Johari Window
  2. Identify the Mayer-Salovey ability model, the research foundation beneath the applied one
  3. Name emotions precisely and use affect labeling to turn reactivity into regulation
  4. Practice active listening, paraphrase, clarify, withhold judgment, against a real rubric
  5. Reframe an accusation into Nonviolent Communication's Observation, Feeling, Need, Request
  6. Raise a hard truth with candor and care, and commit one practice to a real relationship this week

The model

One link. Two editions.
Zero slide decks.

01

Learners scan in

The welcome slide carries a QR code. Learners open the course on their own devices and do every exercise hands-on: simulators, labs, checks, and a capstone plan they keep. Nobody just watches.

02

Anyone can teach it

The facilitator edition scripts every slide: what to say, what to do, what to ask, the answers to expect, and how to transition, plus prep checklists and contingencies. First-time facilitators deliver with confidence.

03

The link is the takeaway

No handout to lose. The same URL works in class, over Zoom, and at a desk six months later, with printable cheat sheets built in. Learning transfer starts with material people can find again.
