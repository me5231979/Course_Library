AI Basics

Classroom 60 min · Self-paced ~25 min

Embodies an entrepreneurial spirit and leverages data and technologyArtificial IntelligenceDigital Fluency/Information LiteracyData Security

Never used AI? Start here. Five plain-language ideas with zero jargon: what AI actually is, how to ask it for help in ordinary English, the traffic-light rule for what's safe to paste, the five chores it does best, and a first try you build in the session and run this week.

  • 5 ideas · 4 trainers + prompt builder
  • Classroom 60 min (30 core) · self-paced ~25 min · no prerequisites
  • In person, virtual, or self-paced
Full course description (printable)
Learning objectives
  1. Say what AI is in one plain sentence, and spot it in tools you already use every day
  2. Ask AI for help in ordinary English, and make the answer better with one follow-up
  3. Know the traffic-light rule: what's fine to ask, what needs an approved tool, what never goes in
  4. Name five everyday chores AI can take off your plate
  5. Make your first try this week, with a starter prompt you build in the session and keep

First Drafts, Faster

Classroom one live session · Self-paced ~15 min

Embodies an entrepreneurial spirit and leverages data and technologyBusiness WritingArtificial IntelligenceEditing & Proofreading

Save time and be more efficient in writing and editing communications. One method, brief, draft, edit: the AI writes the first draft and you edit the version that gets sent. Three worked examples (an email, an announcement, a summary), a practice report to run, every claim cited, and a printable one-page plan.

  • 11 pages · 3 worked examples + knowledge check
  • Classroom: one live session · self-paced ~15 min
  • Classroom, facilitator, and self-paced editions
Learning objectives
  1. Brief an AI like a colleague using CRIT: Context, Role, Interview, Task
  2. Produce a usable first draft of an email, announcement, or summary without starting from a blank page
  3. Edit an AI draft into your own: restore your voice, verify every fact, cut what isn’t needed
  4. Route each writing task to the right Vanderbilt tool by data sensitivity
  5. Apply the method to tomorrow’s first piece of writing, and leave with a printed plan

Working Smarter

Classroom 60–90 min · Self-paced 60–90 min

Embodies an entrepreneurial spirit and leverages data and technologyArtificial IntelligencePrompt WritingDigital Fluency/Information Literacy

Learn the new tools by using them. The flagship of the CHART Program moves from what AI is (you watch a model predict the next word) to the CRIT prompt framework, drilled in working practice apps, to choosing the right Vanderbilt tool, ChatGPT EDU, Amplify, or Copilot, by data sensitivity. You finish with a knowledge check and a readiness plan built from your own task list.

  • 9 sections · 4 practice apps + opportunity simulator
  • Classroom 60–90 min · self-paced 60–90 min · every module skippable
  • Classroom, facilitator, and self-paced editions
Full course description (printable)
Learning objectives
  1. Explain what AI tools like ChatGPT are, and why they sometimes state made-up things as fact
  2. Map your own role task by task, and build a 30/60/90 development plan from the tasks AI can help with
  3. Write clear, complete prompts with the CRIT framework: Context, Role, Interview, Task
  4. Choose the right approved tool for any task by data sensitivity: ChatGPT EDU, Amplify, or Copilot
  5. Use AI responsibly: verify outputs, disclose substantive AI help, and keep sensitive data out of public tools

Emotional Intelligence & Interpersonal Skills

Classroom 90 min · Self-paced ~45 min

Grows self and othersRadically collaborates and cultivates belongingConflict ManagementCommunication Strategies

Lead yourself before you lead others: the research case for EI (Goleman, Google's Project Aristotle), the four domains, mapping your blind spots with the Johari Window, the neuroscience of naming emotions, active listening scored against a rubric, Rosenberg's NVC reframe, and saying hard things with candor and care.

  • 7 sections · 7 interactive tools + private Johari mapper
  • Classroom 90 min (60 core) · self-paced ~45 min
  • In person, virtual, or self-paced
Full course description (printable)
Learning objectives
  1. Explain Goleman's four EI domains and map your own blind spots with the Johari Window
  2. Identify the Mayer-Salovey ability model, the research foundation beneath the applied one
  3. Name emotions precisely and use affect labeling to turn reactivity into regulation
  4. Practice active listening, paraphrase, clarify, withhold judgment, against a real rubric
  5. Reframe an accusation into Nonviolent Communication's Observation, Feeling, Need, Request
  6. Raise a hard truth with candor and care, and commit one practice to a real relationship this week

Presentation & Public Speaking

Classroom 90 min · Self-paced ~45 min

Continuously strives for excellencePublic SpeakingStorytellingStrategic CommunicationStakeholder Communications

Building and delivering to a room or a leadership audience: the myth-busting research on what actually separates memorable presenters, Duarte's Big Idea and Sparkline, Minto's SCQA for the executive room, delivery technique from Anderson, Gallo, and the AMA, and composure under pointed questions, with a live, timed speaking rep for every participant.

  • 7 sections · 7 interactive tools + exec briefing lab
  • Classroom 90 min (60 core) · self-paced ~45 min · everyone speaks once
  • In person, virtual, or self-paced
Full course description (printable)
Learning objectives
  1. Explain why gravitas and communication, not technical mastery alone, decide how a leader's message lands, and name your executive-presence growth edge
  2. Distill a real presentation into one resonant sentence with Duarte's Big Idea: point of view plus what's at stake
  3. Structure a talk with Duarte's Sparkline, alternating What Is and What Could Be toward New Bliss
  4. Structure an executive briefing with Minto's Pyramid Principle and SCQA, leading with the answer
  5. Apply the delivery techniques of Anderson, Gallo, and the AMA in a live, timed speaking round
  6. Handle pointed questions with the bridge, and commit to rebuilding one real presentation, opening line written

The model

One link. Two editions.
Zero slide decks.

01

Learners scan in

The welcome slide carries a QR code. Learners open the course on their own devices and do every exercise hands-on: simulators, labs, checks, and a capstone plan they keep. Nobody just watches.

02

Anyone can teach it

The facilitator edition scripts every slide: what to say, what to do, what to ask, the answers to expect, and how to transition, plus prep checklists and contingencies. First-time facilitators deliver with confidence.

03

The link is the takeaway

No handout to lose. The same URL works in class, over Zoom, and at a desk six months later, with printable cheat sheets built in. Learning transfer starts with material people can find again.
