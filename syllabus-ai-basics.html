About this course

A working picture of how AI actually works, built for people who use it at work, not people who build it. The session moves from what AI is (and isn't), through how machines learn and how language models generate answers, to the practical skills: writing prompts that work, verifying output before trusting it, and choosing the right Vanderbilt-approved tool for the sensitivity of the data involved. It closes with each learner building a one-week plan to put AI on one of their own tasks.

Learning objectives

By the end of this session, participants will be able to:

  1. Distinguish AI, machine learning, and generative AI, and spot each in the tools you already use
  2. Explain how a language model produces an answer, and why the same question gives different answers
  3. Construct four-part prompts (role, context, task, format) that get useful, reliable results on real work
  4. Evaluate AI output before trusting it, and route tasks to the right Vanderbilt tool by data sensitivity
  5. Create a one-week plan that puts AI on one of your own tasks, and keeps the time it saves

Topics covered

  • What AI actually is. AI, machine learning, deep learning, and generative AI, untangled with an interactive ring diagram
  • How machines learn. Supervised, unsupervised, and reinforcement learning, practiced in a you-be-the-engineer scenario game
  • Inside a neural network. Layers, weights, and why even builders can't fully explain one decision
  • Language models. Next-token prediction, felt firsthand in a build-a-sentence simulator
  • Steering the output. Temperature and randomness, explored with a live slider
  • Prompting well. The four-part prompt and the Persona Pattern, then practiced in a graded Prompt Lab
  • Strengths and limits. What AI is genuinely good at, where it fails, and what a hallucination is
  • Using AI responsibly. Bias, privacy, accountability, and Vanderbilt's green/yellow/red data rules
  • Keeping the time AI saves. Workload creep, wellbeing, and deciding where the saved hour goes

How you'll learn

Six interactive simulators (concept rings, ML trainer, neural network, token predictor, temperature slider, prompt anatomy), a graded Prompt Lab, inline knowledge checks in every section, a six-question scored recap, and the My AI Plan capstone. Every section pairs teaching with something to do; nothing is watch-only. Private exercises stay on the learner's screen and are never collected.

Outcomes and how learning is measured

Learners leave with a tested four-part prompt structure, a personal verification habit, clarity on which tool (Amplify, Copilot, ChatGPT) fits which data level, and a written one-week plan for applying AI to their own work. Learning is measured in session (knowledge checks and a recap quiz mapped to the objectives) and after (the capstone plan and a follow-up pulse).

Grounded in Vanderbilt's Dr. Jules White's prompt-pattern research, current AI-at-work research on workload intensification (HBR), and Vanderbilt's AI platform guidance.
